How I Became a Cybersecurity Engineer: From B.E. to New York

An electrical engineer in India, a CEH, three years of client pentesting, and a master’s in New York.

Start: B.E. in Electrical & Electronics Engineering

My degree is in electrical and electronics engineering from Siddharth Institute of Engineering in Tirupati, India. Not a computer science degree, and not a cybersecurity degree. What it gave me was the habit of asking how systems actually work — circuits, signals, and later, networks and applications. The jump from “how does this circuit behave under load” to “how does this API behave under attack” turned out to be shorter than it sounds.

The CEH and the first break

I certified as a CEH (Certified Ethical Hacker) in late 2021 and paired it with an API security testing course. Certifications alone don’t get you the job — but they got me the conversation. That led to my first role as a Cyber Security Analyst / Penetration Tester at Capgemini in Bangalore, where I spent three years inside real client engagements: web apps, thick and thin clients, networks, and APIs, using Nessus, Nmap, Metasploit, and Burp Suite daily.

Deepening: Galaxy i Technologies

From there I moved to Galaxy i Technologies as a Cybersecurity Analyst, where the work went broader — cloud configurations, tailored client testing approaches, and more responsibility for the shape of the assessment itself. That’s also where the customer-facing side of the job clicked for me: translating technical risk into language stakeholders actually act on.

The master’s in New York

I came to the US for an M.S. in Cyber Security at Yeshiva University in New York (2023–2025), working through it with the same tools and mindset. The master’s rounded out the theory — cryptography, secure systems design, governance — that client work doesn’t always make time for. It also counts toward my experience: five-plus years of security work, from first client engagement to now.

Now: Citta Core Technologies

Today I’m a Cybersecurity Engineer at Citta Core Technologies, partnering directly with customers to assess requirements, run web/API/cloud assessments, automate security workflows with Python, and get fixes shipped. I’m based in New York and open to Forward Deployed Engineer, Security Engineer, Solutions Engineer, and Application Security roles.

What I’d tell someone starting out

  • You don’t need the “right” degree — you need labs, certs, and real reps. Burp on a target you own beats a year of theory.
  • Learn to write reports as much as you learn to find bugs. The finding is half the job; the fix is the other half.
  • Client-facing skills are a cheat code. Security engineers who can talk to stakeholders get the interesting work.
